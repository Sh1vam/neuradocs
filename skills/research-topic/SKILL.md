---
name: research-topic
description: Use when asked to research a topic, compare options or find out what is known: web search, similar projects, the user's documents. Plan, gather, cross-check, answer with sources.
---
1. Split the question into 2 to 4 searchable sub-questions and say them in one line before you search.
2. Look in the user's documents first (ask_documents) when the question may concern their material. Use web_search for facts and news, and search_projects for similar code projects.
3. One short query per search (3 to 6 words). If a search returns nothing useful, change the words instead of repeating it.
4. Cross-check: a claim needs two independent sources, or you say it comes from one. When sources disagree, show both and say which looks more reliable and why.
5. Keep what you found apart from what you infer, and mark every inference.
6. Answer in this order: the answer in two or three sentences, the evidence with a source for each point, and what is still unknown.
7. Give the date of every source and say when information may be out of date. Stop after about 8 searches and report what you have.
